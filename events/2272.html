OBJECTIVES: Mpox (also known as monkeypox) can cause significant morbidity in immunocompromised individuals, as evidenced in multiple recent outbreaks in non-endemic countries. We assessed orthopoxvirus immunoglobulin G seroprevalence in an at-risk Thai cohort to identify prior exposure and associated clinical and behavioral features. METHODS: Archived 2022 specimens from an acute human immunodeficiency virus cohort were selected based on risk criteria, including multiple partners, a history of sexually transmitted infection, or drug use. Samples were tested using the Meso Scale Discovery V-PLEX Orthopoxvirus immunoglobulin G assay (Meso Scale Discovery, Rockville, MD, USA). Demographic, clinical, and behavioral data were analyzed using Mann-Whitney U and Fisher's exact tests. RESULTS: Of 491 participants, 5.5% (27/491) were seropositive. Seropositivity was detected as early as January 2022, predating Thailand's first clinical case (July 2022) and wastewater detection (June 2022). Only 7.4% (2/27) of seropositive participants had abnormal physical examinations. Illicit drug use (74.1%) and meeting multiple behavioral risk criteria were significantly associated with seropositivity (P < 0.001 and P = 0.0317, respectively). No significant differences were found regarding CD4 counts, human immunodeficiency virus viral load suppression, or concurrent STIs. CONCLUSION: Unrecognized, paucisymptomatic mpox transmission occurred in Thailand months before official detection. These findings highlight the utility of serologic surveillance within established high-risk cohorts for the early detection of emerging infectious threats.

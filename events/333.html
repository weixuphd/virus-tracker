Choi Chang-June, Sung Suah, Kang Yurim, Kim Young Rong, Hyun Hakjun, Kim Eun Jin, Choi Young Hwa, Yoo Jin Sae, Yang Cheongin, Park Ji Eun, Jung Yun Jung, Heo Jung Yeon · International journal of infectious diseases : IJID : official publication of the International Society for Infectious Diseases

OBJECTIVES: We evaluated temporal trends in invasive pulmonary aspergillosis (IPA) incidence in relation to serum galactomannan (GM) testing intensity and compared outcomes by classical host-risk status. METHODS: Adults with ICU stays ≥72 hours during 2017-2024 were retrospectively screened for proven/probable IPA. Consensus EORTC/MSGERC and FUNDICU definitions defined the primary cohort. Modified criteria were exploratory. Negative binomial and Cox regression assessed incidence and 30-day mortality. RESULTS: Among 11,707 ICU stays, 135 patients met consensus definitions; 55 (40.7%) lacked classical host risk factors, rising to 48.9% under modified criteria. IPA incidence increased over time (IRR/year 1.40; 95% CI 1.06-1.85). After adjustment for serum GM testing intensity, the calendar-year IRR was 0.73 (95% CI 0.54-0.98), whereas testing intensity was strongly associated with measured incidence (IRR 6.15; 95% CI 2.81-13.43). Patients without classical host risk factors received antifungal therapy less often (12.7% vs 67.5%) and had higher crude 30-day mortality (58.2% vs 36.2%). The adjusted host-status association was attenuated (aHR 1.48; 95% CI 0.82-2.66). CONCLUSIONS: The temporal rise in measured IPA incidence was associated with serum GM testing intensity, underscoring diagnostic ascertainment. Patients without classical host risk factors were treated less often and had higher mortality, although the adjusted association was attenuated.
